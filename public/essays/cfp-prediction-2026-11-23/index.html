A model says the playoff committee will leave four teams out of its top 25. Their résumés say they belong.

Posted November 23, 2026, the night before the committee's release.

When the College Football Playoff committee announces its rankings Tuesday night, UTSA, Louisiana Tech, New Mexico and North Dakota State will probably not be on the list. Judged only on what they have done on the field this season, all four would be.

That is the forecast of a statistical model built to predict the committee's behavior, and it is being published here before the committee speaks, so that anyone can check it afterward.

Why would the model expect that? Because it learned from the committee itself.

The project behind the prediction

This forecast comes from a study of every ranking the committee has issued: 72 of them, from 2014 through 2025. The study asks a question fans have argued about for a decade. When two teams have the same résumé, does it matter which conference they play in?

To keep the answer honest, the method was written down and frozen before the analysis was allowed to see which conference each team belonged to. The analysis then ran once, and a second program, written independently, had to reproduce the result.

The early finding: the committee has ranked teams from the smaller conferences well below Power-conference teams with the same résumé, by about eight positions on average. Follow-up checks suggest that up to about half of that gap could be the committee correctly reading records that flatter weaker schedules. The rest is harder to explain. The full results will be published here in the coming weeks.

The model that produced tonight's forecast uses the same ingredients: each team's record, schedule strength, quality wins, bad losses and scoring margins through Sunday's games, weighted the way the committee has weighted them for 12 years. It predicts what the committee will do, not what it should do.
